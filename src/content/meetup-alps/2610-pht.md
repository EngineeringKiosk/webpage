    name: 'Sebastian Gstir'
    title: 'Photonic Integrated Circuits - The Next Generation of Microchips?'
    description:
      'Explaining Photonics Integrated Circuits (PICs) as "chip similar to electronic chips, but instead of electricity they work with light" works for quite a few people at a party. But such a strongly simplified description can lead to questions like "When they replace electronic chips?" and "Is that an alternative to quantum computers?". Neither are completely off, but again tricky to answer while keeping it simple.
      The goal of this talk is therefore to give an thorough introduction to PICs and the ecosystem surrounding them. To answer what they are, how and where they are manufactured, what they are used for and how are they related to electronic chips and the semiconductor industry in general. To go beyond the usual simplifications give an overview of an exotic sounding topic and to show that PICs are already way more present in our live than one would expect.'
    bio: 'From the dark photonics lab in the mountains to the high-tech lowlands housing a backbone of our modern world. Sebastian studied Experimental Physics at the University of Innsbruck and specialised in Quantum Optics. In his PhD in the photonics research group of Prof. Gregor Weihs, he performed fundamental research in the area of interference of single photons. Even though, this was - in his own words - a very niche research topic and only very few other groups conduct similar experiments it brought him in contact with another field called Photonic Integrated Circuits (PICs). Having designed one from scratch for an experiment, he decided to become a photonic design engineer at Epiphany B.V., a small design house in the Netherlands. After almost three years working there he got a deep insight into the inner workings of this ecosystem and designed multiple chips for a variety of applications and clients all over the world.'

  - avatar: './images/speaker/2610-sven-schleier.png'
    name: 'Sven Schleier'
    title: 'Spec, Ship, Break, Fix - Building a real world app without writing code and letting AI agents attack and fix it'
    description:
      'For a long time I have done Application Security and Penetration tests and wanted to know how secure an app written by AI really is and how helpful AI can be in identifying and fixing bugs. Instead of relying on yet another "damn-vulnerable" app, I built and published a real app (web+mobile) by following spec-driven development, including security best practices. I wrote the specifications together with AI: the requirements, architecture, and design while the code itself was 100% AI-written.
      Then I tried to break it and I turned AI on the result: Strix as an autonomous pentester, run with both local and frontier models, and Burp AT as a hands-on pentest co-pilot. With a budget of €50, I tracked what each pentest approach actually cost and how mature and efficient this loop of "build-break-fix-with-AI" really is.'
    bio: 'Sven leads the Global Cyber Security Centre of Excellence at SoftwareOne, building professional and managed security services. In addition to his day job, Sven has been involved with the Open Worldwide Application Security Project (OWASP) since 2016. As a co-project lead and author, he has made significant contributions to the OWASP Mobile Application Security (MAS) project which is considered the industry standard for mobile application security, see https://mas.owasp.org. He is also a frequent speaker and trainer at conferences around the world.'
